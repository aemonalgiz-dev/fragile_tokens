The Abandonment Signal

Two untried attacks on glitch-token detection. One died under a well-powered null. The other says you can name the doomed tokens after 256 of 143,000 steps — and that the standard detectors are blind exactly when it would matter.

Direction 01 — falsifiedConfidence is cheap to contradict

The hypothesis: compositional under-training is not the absence of data but the absence of variation. Find n-grams the model has welded into atomic units — where every constituent token is common and healthy — then violate the weld.

The welds are real. A single O(|V|) sweep over the vocabulary found prerequ→isites at p=0.994, pione→ers at p=0.997, and a memorised citation template at p=1.000. No |V|n search required.

Violating them does nothing. Across 1,200 chains spanning commitment from p=0.036 to p=1.000, inserting a token the model assigns p=1e-5 produced a copy-logprob drop uncorrelated with how committed the model was: r = −0.003.

Commitment and fragility are decoupled. A model's confidence structure has no measurable relationship to its robustness structure.

That extends Broken Tokens from “models tolerate non-canonical tokenizations” to the stronger “models tolerate maximal violation of their own most confident predictions.”

Direction 02 — confirmedDoomed tokens are legible at step 256

Ground truth here is behavioural, not an embedding heuristic: the same validated copy probe run over all 50,304 tokens of the final model, worst 1% = glitch. Features come only from checkpoint t; a logistic regression over eight embedding statistics is fit on half the vocabulary and scored on the held-out half.

The early signal is gradient sparsity, not clairvoyance

One indicator carries everything early — the token-specific residual of the input embedding update (AUC 0.842 at step 2, everything else at chance). Input-embedding gradients are sparse: only tokens actually present in a batch get a token-specific update. So this is corpus-free frequency estimation read off the optimizer. Useful — it needs no corpus counts and no behavioural testing — but it is not the model knowing anything.

Raw update magnitude actively hides the mechanism

The obvious question is whether glitch rows are simply never updated. Measured directly, they appear to be updated as much as healthy rows — ratio ≥ 1.0 through step 512 in both models. That parity is an illusion.

The residual ratio at step 32 is 0.727 on 160m and 0.736 on 1.4b — essentially the same number across a 9× size difference. The masking effect is not a small-model artifact.

This settles a live disagreement. The folk model — glitch tokens are rows that never received gradient — is wrong; they receive large updates. Hub of Short Rows is right that the rows were updated, and this says what those updates were: shared drift, not learning.

About half the glitch set is stillborn — never appears, zero token-specific gradient. The other half appears, receives gradient, and degrades anyway. That second population is invisible to any “was it updated?” test, and it is the one the residual signal catches.

What this doesn't show. Aggregate ratio separation at step 32 is not per-token discriminability — individual prediction still needs step 256. Severity and earliness track together (worst 0.5% reach AUC 0.95 at step 2; worst 5% not until step 2000), so “detectable early” means the severe tail first. And the zero-update fractions go to 0.00% after step 128, which is almost certainly optimizer-state numerics rather than real learning.
